# Sources: What If You Breathe AQI 500 for a Year?

This video shows risks, not a prediction of what one year at AQI 500 would do to one person. No source says that.

| Claim in the video | Source |
|---|---|
| Hazardous AQI begins at 225.5 µg/m³ PM2.5 (used for "over ten cigarettes a day") | US EPA, [PM NAAQS and AQI fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-02/pm-naaqs-air-quality-index-fact-sheet.pdf), 2024 breakpoints |
| About 22 µg/m³ of PM2.5 equals one cigarette per day. A rough, contested rule of thumb (shown as "ROUGH ESTIMATE") | Berkeley Earth, [Air Pollution and Cigarette Equivalence](https://berkeleyearth.org/air-pollution-and-cigarette-equivalence). Its authors call it "crude". Arden Pope's estimate is much lower. 225.5 / 22 is about 10.2 per day, and 10 x 365 = 3,650 |
| PM2.5 can enter the bloodstream and reach major organs. It is linked to stroke, lung cancer, COPD and cardiovascular disease | WHO, [What are the WHO air quality guidelines?](https://www.who.int/news-room/feature-stories/detail/what-are-the-who-air-quality-guidelines) |
| 43.5% of Delhi schoolchildren had reduced lung function, against 25.7% in villages (2008 survey, Central Pollution Control Board) | [Dialogue Earth](https://dialogue.earth/en/pollution/air-pollution-causing-irreversible-damage-to-indias-urban-children/). The video shows 43.5 and 25.7 of every 100 figures lit |
| Living in Delhi's 2023 average PM2.5 (88.4 µg/m³) costs about 8.2 years of life expectancy. This is lifetime exposure, not one year | University of Chicago AQLI, [Delhi report](https://aqli.epic.uchicago.edu/post/delhi-residents-losing-8-2-years-of-their-lives-to-high-air-pollution-report-en) |
| About 1.718 million deaths in India in 2022 were attributed to PM2.5 (shown as 1 dot = 1,000 deaths) | Lancet Countdown on Health and Climate Change, as reported by [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/health/pm-25-not-only-killed-17-million-people-in-india-during-2022-but-also-caused-financial-losses-to-the-tune-of-95-of-countrys-gdp-lancet) |

Simplified or illustrative: the AQI value on the phone, the body fly-through, the calendar and cigarette pile, and the hourglass are visual models, not measurements. The video is not medical advice.
