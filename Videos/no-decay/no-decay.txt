michael: What if, starting right now, *nothing* decayed?

michael: At first, it's great. This apple stays *perfect*. Bread never molds. Milk never spoils.

michael: Then you step outside. Autumn leaves fall, and they *stay*. Nothing softens them. Nothing eats them.

michael: Every year, land plants lock away about *sixty billion tons* of carbon. Normally, fungi and bacteria hand most of it back. Now they've stopped.

michael: So the pile *grows*. Fallen trees stack across the forest floor. Dead animals lie where they dropped. They don't rot. They just stay.

michael: But under your feet, something worse is happening. Plants rely on *nitrogen* that decomposers free from dead matter. Now it's trapped inside the pile. Leaves turn yellow. Forests go hungry.

michael: Then the air starts to change. Plants keep pulling carbon dioxide down, and far less comes back. On simple math, ignoring the oceans, many plants could hit their survival limit in about *ten years*.

michael: The main recycler left is *fire*. One lightning strike, and years of dry litter go up at once.

michael: Decay looks like the end of things. It's actually how the next thing gets *built*.

michael: So what would you want to never decay?
