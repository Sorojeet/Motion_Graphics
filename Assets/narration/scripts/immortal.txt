michael: What if you could *never* die? Not a longer life. No end at all.
michael: At first, it feels like a *gift*. Every language. Every place. No deadlines. No fear of running out of time.
michael: Today, the average person lives about *seventy-three* years. The longest verified life ever? *One hundred twenty-two*.
michael: You would blow past that record, and *keep going*. A thousand years is *eight* times the longest life on record.

michael: But everyone you love would grow old, and *leave*. Again, and again, and again. Even their faces would *fade*.
michael: Cities would rise and fall. Whole languages would *vanish*. Every country you knew would become a *footnote*.
michael: In about half a billion years, Earth may no longer support most plants. In about a *billion*, its oceans could boil away.
michael: Then, roughly *five billion* years from now, the Sun swells into a *red giant*. Earth is not expected to survive.

michael: But you would. Drifting through space, *alive*, in the dark, with nothing left to see.

michael: Nothing could end you. Nothing could *change* you. Maybe what gives life meaning, is that it *ends*. Every moment counts, because it's *limited*.

michael: So, would you *take* it?
